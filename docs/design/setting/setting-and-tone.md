# Setting and tone

Feudum is set in an invented medieval world that is plausible in everything but its magic, and harsh to live in. This page sets the rules every content topic checks against: what belongs in the world, how it behaves, and how it feels.

## World

The world, its realms, and its peoples are invented. Real history and cultures serve as inspiration, never as places or peoples the player meets by name.

## Realism

The world works the way nature and history would make it work. That holds for:

- **Geology**: ores and stone lie where they would form, such as bog iron in wetlands or tin near granite.
- **Climate**: biomes follow latitude, altitude, rain shadows, and coasts.
- **Crafts**: production follows real processes, such as smelting iron in a bloomery or tanning hides with bark.
- **Society**: feudal ranks, duties, and law work as they did.

Gameplay abstracts scale and time but keeps the processes real. A tree may grow in days instead of decades, and a village may count dozens of people instead of hundreds.

Magic is the one departure from realism. It follows consistent rules of its own, and its effects on nature and society follow plausibly from them.

## Magic and mythical creatures

Feudum is meant to be a high-magic world, but magic waits for a design of its own: [Magic](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/201). Until then, content stays mundane, and no design rules magic out.

Mythical creatures, such as dragons, griffins, and wyverns, belong in the world. Like any other creature, each one is an animal with a habitat, prey, and a place in the food chain. Their design waits for magic and [fauna](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/196): [Mythical creatures](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/202).

## Era

The world draws on the High and Late Middle Ages, from about 1000 to 1500, and its technology stops around 1400. That ceiling includes plate armor, blast furnaces, mechanical clocks, spinning wheels, eyeglasses, and carracks. Gunpowder and the printing press stay out of reach.

A village progresses through the era. It starts with high medieval technology, such as timber buildings, bloomeries, and mail, and works its way up to late medieval technology, such as stone buildings, blast furnaces, and plate. How progression works belongs to the [player's role and core loop](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/199).

## Cultures

The world holds several regional cultures, inspired by medieval Europe and its neighbors: Western feudal, Norse, Slavic, Iberian and Mediterranean, Byzantine, North African, and Near Eastern. Each one lives in the climate region of its real counterpart, such as Norse-like cultures in the cold north and Mediterranean-like ones in the warm south. The cultures themselves wait for a design of their own: [Regional cultures](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/203).

## Peoples and faith

Humans are the only people for now. Others may join them later: [Non-human peoples](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/204).

The world has faiths of its own, invented rather than taken from real religions: [Religion](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/205).

## Tone

The world is harsh, cruel, and dirty, with dry humor in the gaps.

- **Violence** is graphic: blood spatter, visible wounds, corpses that stay and rot, and heads on pikes. A client-side option tones it down to mild: a hit flash, a few drops of blood, and bodies that fade.
- **Hardship** sits between hard and bleak. A village can thrive but is never safe: disasters strike often and hard, and deaths and losses are permanent. A village left untended slides into decline, but a well-run one does not decline by default.
- **Morality** sits between gray and grimdark. Lords can be cruel and clergy corrupt, and the world shows torture, massacres, and slavery openly. The player may rule justly or cruelly, through harsh punishment, heavy taxes, or raiding, and every choice carries consequences. How that plays belongs to the [player's role and core loop](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/199).
- **Grit** follows the state of a village. A well-run village looks lived-in, with mud, smoke, and worn tools. A poor or neglected one turns gritty, with filth, rot, rats, and visible disease. Towns, camps, and battlefields beyond the player's village sit at the gritty end.
- **Humor** is dry gallows humor, such as wry flavor text and villagers' dark jokes about their lot, never slapstick or parody.

## Audience and limits

Feudum is meant for adults and older teens, and its README and mod pages warn up front of graphic violence and mature themes.

However dark the world gets, three lines hold:

- No sexual violence, shown or implied.
- No violence against children on screen. Children may still die of famine or disease offscreen.
- No real-world peoples or faiths as targets. The world's cruelty belongs to the world itself.
