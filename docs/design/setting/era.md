# Era

Which period of history Feudum draws on, and how strictly it holds to it. What fantasy adds to the world belongs to [Fantasy](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/220), and whether villages advance through the technology of the era belongs to [Technology progression](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/221).

## The period

Feudum draws on the High Middle Ages in Western Europe, from 1000 to 1300: the height of feudalism and the manor, and of the fief that gives Feudum its name. Western Europe here means Latin Christendom, the same region whose daylight [Time](../world/time.md) follows.

## The rule

Feudum's mundane content (its tools, crafts, buildings, weapons, and inventions) comes from what Western Europeans used between 1000 and 1300.

- Something counts when it was established in use in Western Europe by 1300, as historians usually date it, wherever it was invented. A single early mention, a prototype, or one local center is not enough.
- The period is a floor as well as a ceiling: what had fallen out of use by 1000 is out too.
- The rule allows content without requiring it, so a design may leave out something the period had.
- A design may admit something from outside the period when its gameplay needs it. That design names what it admits and why, so exceptions stay few and easy to find.

Each design judges its own content against the rule. Fantasy content falls outside it.

## Landmarks

The table sorts the landmarks a design is most likely to reach for, borderline cases included. Roman concrete and hypocaust heating fell out of use before 1000; the rest of the right column arrived or spread after 1300.

| Kind                    | In the period                                                                                                     | Out of the period                                               |
|:------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Farming and crafts      | Heavy plow, horse collar, three-field rotation, water mill, post windmill, treadle loom, spinning wheel, bloomery | Blast furnace                                                   |
| Building                | Stone castles, Romanesque and Gothic building, stained glass, wheelbarrow                                         | Roman concrete, hypocaust heating                               |
| Weapons and armor       | Chain mail, crossbow, longbow, counterweight trebuchet                                                            | Plate armor, gunpowder, cannon, firearms                        |
| Instruments and writing | Astrolabe, pocket sundial, magnetic compass                                                                       | Mechanical clocks, sandglass, spectacles, paper mills, printing |
