# Materials and resources

The world yields woods, stones, earths, ores, fuels, fibers, foods, gems, dyes, and other products, each one Feudum's own in place of its vanilla counterpart. This page lists them, what they refine into, and how they compare within their family. Which biomes hold which resources belongs to [Biomes](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/194).

## Scope

The page covers the raw materials the world yields and the materials they refine into, such as bog iron into wrought iron or flax into linen. Finished goods, such as tools, garments, and dishes, stay out. The workshops and trades that run each process belong to [Professions](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/197) and [Buildings](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/198).

The lists are open. They grow as professions, biomes, and other topics call for materials of their own.

Every material is mundane. Magical materials wait for [Magic](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/201).

## What makes the list

- **Real names.** Materials are natural things, so they keep their real names, such as oak, hematite, or madder.
- **A use or a look of its own.** A material earns its place with a use no other member of its family covers as well, or with a distinct look. A cosmetic variety shares its material's row and properties, such as red, yellow, and gray sandstone. A look that behaves differently gets a row of its own.
- **Climate reach.** Every material occurs somewhere in the world, within the climates its [cultures](../setting/setting-and-tone.md#cultures) span, from the cold north to the hot desert. Tropical goods, such as pepper and cinnamon, and New World crops, such as potatoes and maize, join only if [Biomes](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/194) gives them a place to grow.

## How materials rank

No ladder runs across families. Each family rates its members on a few properties of its own, on a three-step scale of low, medium, and high, and a material ranks within its family for each use: oak is strong but slow to work, and pine is quick to work but weaker. Numbers wait for implementation.

Progression gates refined materials by process, not by rank. A village reaches a material once it can run the process that makes it, and the refining tables mark the processes that belong to the late medieval end of the [era](../setting/setting-and-tone.md#era). How progression plays belongs to the [player's role and core loop](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/199).

Gold, silver, and gems rank by value, which belongs to [Trade and economy](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/209).

## Renewal and deposits

Plants and animals renew: trees regrow, crops are sown again, and herds breed. Minerals do not. Each deposit of stone, earth, ore, peat, or coal runs dry once worked out, and the village then finds a new deposit or trades for the material.

Ore deposits come in three grades:

| Grade    | Yield  | Pays in                                                 |
|:---------|:-------|:--------------------------------------------------------|
| Rich     | High   | Any process                                             |
| Middling | Medium | Any process                                             |
| Lean     | Low    | Late medieval processes only, such as the blast furnace |

A bloomery loses much of its iron to slag, so lean ore only pays once the village runs a process that gets more metal out of it. A deposit not worth working early becomes worth working later. The size and placement of deposits belong to [Biomes](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/194).

| Tallow    |        | Low         | Candles, rushlights, soap, greasing                  |

## Dyes

Dyes rate on fastness, and each gives a color. Most need alum as a mordant to fix them. The five cover blue, red, and yellow, and mix to the rest: woad and weld give green, madder and weld orange, and woad and madder purple.

| Dye     | Color         | Fastness | Source                    |
|:--------|:--------------|:---------|:--------------------------|
| Woad    | Blue          | High     | Leaves of the woad plant  |
| Madder  | Red           | High     | Roots of the madder plant |
| Weld    | Yellow        | High     | The whole weld plant      |
| Kermes  | Scarlet       | High     | Insects on the kermes oak |
| Lichens | Purple, brown | Low      | Lichens on rock and bark  |
