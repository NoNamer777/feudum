# Tools

Which hand tools players and villagers work with, what each does, and how it wears. [Era](../setting/era.md) sets which tools belong to the period, and [Plants](../farming/plants.md#harvest) the tool each plant is harvested with. How tools are made, and what makes one better than another, belongs to [Crafts](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/247).

## What counts as a tool

This page covers the hand tools a player or villager carries and uses on the world, in field, woodland, earth, and stone, along with the bucket. Other gear belongs elsewhere:

- **Craftsmen's tools and workshop equipment**, such as the smith's tongs, the carpenter's saw, the anvil, and the loom, belong to [Crafts](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/247).
- **Implements drawn by animals**, such as the plow and the harrow, belong to [Fields](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/244).
- **Weapons** belong to [Weapons](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/265). An axe or knife can still strike in a fight, and how well it fights belongs there too.

## Materials

Each tool takes the form the period used. The cutting and digging tools have an iron head on a wooden handle, or for the spade, a wooden blade with an iron shoe, and the shears are iron throughout. The pitchfork and rake are wood throughout, and the flail is two wooden staves joined by a leather thong. The [whetstone](#honing-and-grinding) and the [bucket](#the-bucket) take the forms set below. Feudum has no ladder of materials like vanilla's wood, stone, iron, and diamond; tools differ in [quality](#quality) instead. Stone and flint cutting tools had fallen out of use by 1000, so the [era rule](../setting/era.md#the-rule) keeps them out.

Each tool has a job of its own, in its real period form. The table lists them by the work they do, with the hands they take:

| Mattock   | Field    | Two   | Breaking hard ground, and grubbing out roots and stumps    |
| Pitchfork | Field    | Two   | Lifting hay, straw, and sheaves                            |
| Rake      | Field    | Two   | Gathering hay and straw                                    |

A two-handed tool keeps the off hand empty while it is held. The spade does the work of a shovel, and the billhook and axe that of a hatchet, so neither has a tool of its own. Saws belong to Crafts, quarrying wedges to [Mining](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/263), and fishing gear to [Fishing](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/267). How the hoe works a field belongs to Fields, threshing as a step in making food to [Food](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/246), and sheep to [Animals](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/228).

## Using a tool

Each job has a right tool and, where the period had one, a second-best tool. The right tool works fastest and yields most. A second-best tool works slower and yields less unless the table says otherwise, and bare hands slower and less again. Any tool not listed for a job counts as bare hands. Work the period did by hand, such as picking fruit, needs no tool. Testing tunes the gaps.

A few jobs are impossible without their tool, such as felling a grown tree or breaking rock. The table lists each job with its tools and what bare hands manage:

## Wear

Tools wear in two ways, as they did in the period: the edge dulls with use, and the tool itself wears out over long use. Testing tunes how fast each happens.

### The edge

Every tool with a working edge or point dulls with use: the sickle, scythe, hoe, mattock, spade, axe, billhook, pick, knife, and shears. As it dulls, work slows and yields fall, smoothly, as [fatigue's effects](../needs/fatigue.md#effects) grow. Four labels name points on the way: Keen, Sharp, Dull, and Blunt. A blunt tool also sounds and looks different when it strikes. The pitchfork, rake, flail, and bucket have no edge and never dull.

A dull tool builds no extra fatigue. The job takes longer, and the longer [exertion](../needs/fatigue.md#building-fatigue) already builds more.

### Honing and grinding

A dulled edge comes back in two ways:

- **Whetstone**: carried and used anywhere, such as in the field. It quickly hones a dull edge back to Sharp and costs the tool nothing, but cannot restore a blunt one.
- **Grindstone**: a block a village builds. It brings any edge back to Keen, blunt ones included, but each grinding takes metal off the head. How it is made belongs to Crafts.

The whetstone wears thinner with each honing until it is too small to use, and then it is replaced. Whetstones come from fine-grained stone: where it is found belongs to Mining, and how it is cut to Crafts.

### The head and the handle

The head never breaks suddenly. It wears thinner with use and with each grinding, through the labels Sound, Worn, and Worn thin. A smith re-steels a worn head to bring it back, and once it is worn past mending, its iron goes back to the smith as scrap. Which smithing trades exist belongs to Crafts.

The handle wears with use and at last breaks suddenly, but it shows cracks and creaks under use before it does. Fitting a new handle is quick work. Making handles belongs to Crafts.

### The bucket

A bucket holds what [Water behavior](../world/water-behavior.md#buckets) sets. It is made of wooden staves bound with hoops of wood or iron, as in the period, and Feudum has no metal or leather buckets. It never dulls, but over long use its hoops loosen and it leaks until a cooper re-hoops it. Coopering belongs to Crafts.

## Quality

A better tool holds its edge longer and lasts longer before it needs a smith. It also works a little faster, but having the right tool matters far more than having a good one. How quality comes about, such as from a steel edge or the smith's skill, and the names of its grades belong to Crafts.

## Reading a tool

A tool's tooltip shows its quality, its edge, and its head, each as a label. Nothing shows wear as a number or a bar.

## Getting tools

On first joining a world, each player receives a knife, axe, spade, sickle, and whetstone of ordinary quality. The whetstone keeps the kit sharp until the player has a grindstone. The kit comes only once: a player who respawns gets no new one, and what they keep on death belongs to [Death](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/266).

Beyond the kit, a player trades for tools with [generated villages](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/240) until their own village has a smith. How a village grows its own smithing belongs to [Technology progression](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/221).

## Villagers

Villagers work with tools from the village's stores, under the same rules as players, and wear them the same way, so a village short of sickles reaps slower at harvest. They hone their tools with a whetstone as they work, take blunt ones to the grindstone, and take worn heads to the smith. A village without a smith sends its tools out through trade. When villagers do this belongs to [Villager schedules](https://github.com/NoNamer777/feudum/issues/214).

## Vanilla tools

Feudum's tools replace vanilla's, so vanilla's tools and its iron bucket are hidden everywhere. Enchanting is off, which takes Mending and Unbreaking with it, and anvils repair nothing. Feudum's grindstone is a block of its own, not vanilla's. The tooltip labels take the place of the durability bar.
