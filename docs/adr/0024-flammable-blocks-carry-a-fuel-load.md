# Flammable blocks carry a fuel load

Every flammable block carries a fuel load, which sets how long it feeds a fire, and an ignition threshold, which sets how easily it catches; a fire burns down the load and leaves the block's burnt form. How easily a block catches also depends on its dryness, which is computed from the recent weather at its place and whether it is covered, not stored on each block. Vanilla's fire would have been simpler: a fire block that burns for a random time and may delete its neighbor, so a log and a leaf burn alike. But a village fire matters because of how long things burn: thatch flares up and is gone, and a timber hall burns for hours. The hearth, the oven, the kiln, and the bath furnace also need one measure of fuel to share. Computing dryness from the weather, as [ADR 0004](0004-feudum-owns-the-weather.md) makes possible, lets a soaked roof resist sparks and a dry summer turn grassland into tinder without saving a wetness on every block in the world. The cost is that every flammable block in Feudum's content must declare a fuel load, an ignition threshold, and a burnt form. Every burning block saves its remaining load, and dryness must be sampled from the weather wherever a fire might spread.
