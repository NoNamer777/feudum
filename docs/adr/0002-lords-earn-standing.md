# Lords earn standing, not ownership

A player never owns a village. Each village keeps a standing for every player it knows, and lordship is the top of that scale: the village offers it, and takes it back when standing falls, the lord lapses, or a contest is lost. Formal ownership would have been simpler, with one player claiming a village and directing it outright. But it would have made the village a tool rather than a community, and left lordless villages with no way to choose whom to follow. Standing lets a village steer itself while a trusted player leads it, and it gives every player a say in proportion to that trust. The cost is that every mechanic which helps or harms a village must define how it moves standing, and that a player cannot simply take a village they want.
