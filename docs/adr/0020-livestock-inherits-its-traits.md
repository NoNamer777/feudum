# Livestock inherits its traits

Every animal carries heritable trait values for size, yield, hardiness, temperament, and color, and each young animal takes the midpoint of its parents' values plus a random variation. A breed is a data-driven set of trait ranges with a look and a home region, so an animal's breed follows from its traits. Fixed breed labels, where like begets like, would have been simpler to build and to read. But they would have left nothing for a keeper to improve, and no path from a wild boar to a farmyard pig. Medieval breeds were regional types shaped by generations of keeping the best stock, not fixed kinds. Trait values let selective breeding move a line, let domestication be temperament rising over generations, and give Textiles the natural fleece colors of its breeds. The cost is that every species from here on must carry trait ranges and breeds in its livestock profile. Every mechanic that reads an animal's size, yield, hardiness, or temperament must read its traits, and Village simulation must pass them on to the young of an unloaded village.
