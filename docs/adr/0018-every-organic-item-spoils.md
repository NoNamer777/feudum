# Every organic item spoils

Every organic item carries a keeping time and spoils through the stages Fresh, Stale, Spoiled, and Rotten, at a rate set by temperature, wetness, and its container. That covers food and fodder, and also raw hides, raw fleece, cut stalks and herbs, seeds, saplings, and carcasses. Only processing that stabilized a material historically, such as tanning or spinning, ends it. Limiting spoilage to perishable food would have been simpler, and would have left grain, flour, and raw materials free to stockpile. But a medieval village lived by the calendar of its stores: hides went to the tanner within days, fleece was washed before the moths found it, and hay rotted in a wet rick. One rule makes timing, cold, and dry storage matter for everything a village takes from the land, and leaves the differences to the values. The cost is that every content design from here on must give each organic item a keeping time and a rotten version. Every design that makes one organic item from another must carry spoilage through, every such item and block must save its keeping time, and Village simulation must spoil the stock of an unloaded village.
