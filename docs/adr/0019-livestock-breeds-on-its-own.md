# Livestock breeds on its own

Livestock conceives whenever a fertile female in season and a fertile male of her species share a space, both are well fed, and neither is crowded. No player action starts it. Vanilla breeding, where hand-feeding a pair starts love mode, would have kept herds under the player's exact control. But it made every calf the result of a click, and a herd left alone never changed. A medieval herd grew or dwindled by its keeping: the rams turned in with the ewes in autumn, the fodder that lasted the winter, and the bulls kept apart or gelded. Breeding on its own makes a keeper's work the managing of who runs together, and lets a village's herds grow while no one is near. The cost is that herds can outgrow their land, so every species needs a crowding area and every world a cap on livestock per chunk. Village simulation must also breed the herds of an unloaded village by the same conditions.
