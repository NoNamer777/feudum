# Water is finite and volumetric

Every water block holds a volume of up to 1000 liters that flows, levels out, evaporates, and soaks away, and no rule creates water from nothing. Vanilla's two-source rule would have been far cheaper, and so would finite sources that large bodies refill. But Thirst makes every liter a body drinks matter, and only real volume lets a pond drop after a summer of watering a herd, a sand pit drain unless clay lines it, a dry season empty a shallow well, or a flood spill onto the fields. Feudum builds water from scratch, swimming and buoyancy included, rather than reusing vanilla's, so nothing in it assumes the vanilla flow. The cost is simulation. Water that has leveled out must settle and wake only for a change nearby, ocean water at sea level must hold its level as a fixed boundary, a per-world tick budget must cap the work, and unloaded chunks must catch up in one step when they load. Every system that adds or takes water, from containers and wells to Weather's rain and melt, must move real liters.
