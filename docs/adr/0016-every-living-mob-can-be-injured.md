# Every living mob can be injured

Every living mob, players included, carries the health of Feudum: pools for the parts of its body, a blood value, injuries, and healing driven by its needs, tuned by a data-driven body profile for its entity type. Limiting injuries to players and villagers would have been simpler, since animals could have kept vanilla's hit points and healing. But a management game lives on its livestock as well as its villagers. One model makes a lamed horse, a wolf bleeding from an arrow, or an ox healing slowly through a lean winter matter as much as a wounded guard. The cues on a herd tell the player how it fares. The cost is that every mob carries its pools, blood, and injuries while loaded, and every new entity type needs a body profile or an explicit exemption. Wildlife must give every animal attack a damage kind that injuries can follow, as Weapons does for weapons, and Village simulation must define what an unloaded village does with its wounded.
