# Dishes compute their food component from their ingredients

A dish is cooked from a data-driven template, such as pottage, stew, or pie, whose slots take ingredients from item tags, and its food component is the sum of what went in, adjusted by the cooking method. Fixed recipes would have been simpler: every dish an authored item with a fixed food component, as vanilla's stews are. But a medieval pottage was made of whatever the garden, the field, and the larder gave that day, and a fixed recipe either forbids that or hides it behind a list of near-identical items. Computing from contents makes food groups follow the ingredients, so a pottage of beans and cabbage feeds protein and vegetables, and a village short of fruit can't cook its way out of the deficiency. It also lets a data pack add a dish by adding a template rather than every combination of it. The cost is that a dish item carries its template, ingredients, and computed component as data, that stacking must compare ingredients, and that every ingredient in Foods needs a raw and a cooked food component that Cooking can sum. Plain cooked items, such as a roast joint or a loaf of bread, keep a fixed cooked component.
