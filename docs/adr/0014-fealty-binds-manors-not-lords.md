# Fealty binds manors, not lords

Fealty holds one manor of another, its liege manor, rather than binding one lord to another, and the bond stays with the manors when either changes hands. A realm is the tree of manors under a root manor, so a lord whose manors sit in different realms serves each of them. Personal fealty, with each lord sworn to one liege, would have been simpler: every lord would sit in one realm, and a realm would be a tree of people. But manors are already the unit of lordship ([ADR 0003](0003-lordship-attaches-to-manors.md)), and medieval tenure followed the land the same way: a fief was held of an honour, and its tenants went on holding of the honour when it changed hands. Binding manors lets a lord be a sovereign in one realm and a vassal in another, and lets a contest move a manor without breaking the realm around it. The cost is that a lord's duties can clash, which liege realms settle for aid, and that every realm rule, from tribute to peace within a realm, applies per manor rather than per lord.
