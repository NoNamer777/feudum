# Crops grow by the conditions at their block

Every crop grows by the conditions at its block: the local temperature, the soil moisture, the daylight, and the soil it stands in. Tilled soil stores three nutrients, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, and an acidity. Harvests draw the nutrients down and the scarcest one caps growth and yield, while soil moisture is computed from the weather and the water table rather than stored. A crop stores its growth progress and the time it last caught up, and when its chunk loads it catches up by sampling the weather over the time that passed. Vanilla's crops would have been simpler: random ticks advance a stage wherever there is light and water nearby, so a crop grows the same in winter as in summer and a field never wears out. But a farming year matters to a village only if the season, the weather, and the ground decide the harvest. Winter wheat has to survive the frost, a drought has to cut the yield, and a field sown with wheat every year has to give less until it is manured, rotated, or rested. Computing moisture from the weather, as [ADR 0004](0004-feudum-owns-the-weather.md) makes possible, and soil texture from the seed lets the land shape farming without storing a value on every block in the world. Separate nutrients cost more to read and to balance than a single fertility value, but they make legumes, manure, ash, and bone each matter for a reason of their own. The cost is that every tilled block saves its nutrients and acidity, every crop must sample the weather as it catches up, and world generation must offer a soil texture for every column from the seed alone.
