# Seasons

The year passes through four seasons: spring, summer, autumn, and winter. They change the world and set the rhythm of village life: when to sow, when to harvest, and how much to store before winter.

## Calendar

Each season splits into early, mid, and late, so the world changes gradually rather than overnight. The length of a sub-season is configurable. The default is 8 in-game days, which makes a season 24 days and a year 96 days.

The calendar is the same across the world. What a season does depends on the climate of the biome.

## World effects

In temperate biomes, the seasons change the world as follows:

| Effect      | Spring                          | Summer                    | Autumn                         | Winter                                 |
|:------------|:--------------------------------|:--------------------------|:-------------------------------|:---------------------------------------|
| Day length  | Growing                         | Longest                   | Shrinking                      | Shortest                               |
| Temperature | Mild                            | Warm                      | Cooling                        | Cold: snow falls and water freezes     |
| Weather     | Frequent rain                   | Dry spells, thunderstorms | Rain and fog                   | Snow                                   |
| Foliage     | Fresh green, blossoms           | Deep green                | Leaves turn color and fall     | Bare deciduous trees                   |
| Crops       | Planting season                 | Growth peaks              | Harvest; frost ends late crops | No outdoor growth, except winter crops |
| Animals     | Breeding season, more offspring | Normal                    | Fattening                      | Breeding slows                         |

## Climate by biome

Each biome follows the climate that fits it:

| Climate   | Biomes                               | Seasons                                                                              |
|:----------|:-------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Temperate | Plains, forests, swamps, meadows     | The four seasons in the table above.                                                 |
| Cold      | Taigas, snowy plains, mountain peaks | A long winter and a short, cool summer. Snow covers the ground most of the year.     |
| Arid      | Deserts, badlands                    | Hot, dry summers and mild winters. Rain is rare, and nights are cold all year.       |
| Tropical  | Jungles, savannas, mangrove swamps   | Warm all year. A wet season in summer and autumn, a dry season in winter and spring. |

The Nether and the End have no seasons.

## Village effects

- **Surviving winter.** The village has to store food for the months nothing grows, and gather firewood to heat its homes. Villagers without food starve, and villagers in unheated homes fall ill and freeze. A village that runs out of both can die out.
- **Seasonal work.** Each role's schedule follows the season. Farmers plow and sow in spring and harvest in autumn, woodcutters fell trees in winter, and builders work through summer.
- **Festivals.** Feasts mark the turning points of the year, such as a harvest festival and a midwinter feast. They raise morale and use up surplus food.

## Player effects

The season sets the air temperature, which players feel through the temperature mechanic: they need warm clothing and shelter in winter and shade in summer. Summer heat also makes players thirsty faster, through the thirst mechanic.

## Related mechanics

Seasons work together with these mechanics, which each get a document of their own:

- **Temperature.** Players and villagers feel the air temperature, which the season and biome set.
- **Thirst.** Players need to drink, and need to drink more in the heat.
- **Food spoilage.** Food rots over time unless it is preserved, which makes storing food for winter a real task.

## Additions and changes

- **Seasonal climate.** Biome temperature and weather follow the season and the climate of the biome, and day length varies, which changes the vanilla day and night cycle.
- **Crop seasons.** Every crop gets the seasons it grows in, and new crops fill the seasons vanilla leaves empty, winter included.
- **Almanac.** An item, or a sundial block, that shows the date and the season.
- **Greenhouse.** A greenhouse or cold frame that lets crops grow out of season.
- **Food storage.** Preserving blocks such as a granary, smokehouse, salting barrel, and root cellar, which slow or stop food spoilage.
- **Heating.** Fireplaces that burn firewood to keep villager homes warm.
- **Seasonal foliage.** Leaves that change color, fall in autumn, and leave deciduous trees bare in winter.
